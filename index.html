01

Companies rarely die of a bad product. They die of a bad organization.

That is the whole thesis. Everything else is method.

OSMIN writes the first institutional check into companies whose product could work — and then builds the organization that lets it.

Most seed capital prices the demo, the market slide, and the founder’s last logo. The failure that actually shows up is quieter: no decision rights, no high-potential bench, no operating cadence, a culture that cannot hold the next ten people. The product was never the scarce asset. The system around it was.

We diligence that system with the same seriousness other firms reserve for the molecule or the model. Then we stay in the room long enough to install one.

Most companies don’t fail because of bad products — they fail because of bad organizational design. Dr. Oscar E. Arias, Ed.D. · Managing Director
